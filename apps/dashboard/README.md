# dashboard

Home dashboard at http://dashboard.lab.internal, five pages in the top bar. *Home*, for the whole family: who's home, family calendar (today, then this and next week), package tracking (Posti, Matkahuolto, DHL, UPS), this week's workouts, music (the home BluOS players). *Cameras*, everything camera: *Live* (the default each time the tab opens) shows the home cameras' and the cabin's Protect cameras' live pictures with the cabin 4G camera's latest motion picture beside them; *Detections* (with today's event count) shows motion events and their clips from the cabin 4G camera, the cabin's Protect cameras and the home cameras. *Cabin*: the cabin's heat pumps and its music player. *Telescope*: the cabin's astrophotography (ZWO ASIAIR Plus): the latest frame large (tap for a full-screen viewer over the recent frames), the imaging session and plan progress (with the frame being exposed and what the ASIAIR is busy with), guiding, sky quality over the session, the recent frames, and *Tonight at the cabin* (darkness, moon, clouds and dew); `/?tab=telescope` opens it directly (the telescope's notifications link there); above it a link *Open telescope app* ([apps/telescope/web](../telescope/web/): live stack, guiding, frame rejection, conditions, observing log) to the address matching the dashboard's own: http://telescope.lab.internal at home, https://telescope.shillyshally.org (Cloudflare Access, the same *Family* login) through dashboard.shillyshally.org. *Homelab*: live internet traffic, speed tests, homelab health and backup status, network status and the top 10 devices, under a link to *Components, network and devices* at http://homelab.lab.internal ([apps/homelab](../homelab/README.md): the architecture diagrams, devices, plans and docs; `HOMELAB_APP_URL`). Every page but *Home* returns to *Home* after 2 minutes untouched. All show the clock, weather (tap a location for its 10-day forecast), internet and backup status pills (tap the backup pill for *Homelab*) and night mode. Dates are Finnish, other text English. Built for an iPad (landscape fits one screen; portrait and phones scroll); add it to the Home Screen for full-screen use.

SvelteKit 2 + Svelte 5, adapter-node. `server.js` wraps the SvelteKit handler and relays live camera WebSockets; `session.js` is shared by both.

| UniFi Protect on the UDM Pro (`PROTECT_URL`) and on the cabin's UCG Max (`CABIN_PROTECT_URL`, over the Site Magic VPN); one login for both, `PROTECT_USER`/`PROTECT_PASS` from secret `dashboard-protect` | *Cameras → Detections*, Home cameras and Cabin cameras cards (`/api/protect/home`, `/api/protect/cabin`): Protect's motion and smart-detection events of the last 48 h (at most 1000; the cabin's cameras reach that in about 13 h of wind), by day, with thumbnail, camera, time, length and detections (person, vehicle, animal); filter by camera and detection; tap one for the viewer, which plays the recording. Labels and order from `PROTECT_CAMERAS` and `CABIN_PROTECT_CAMERAS` (Protect name:label:CAMERAS id). **Recordings** in the live viewer of every camera with a CAMERAS id: a timeline under the video (1 h, 6 h or 24 h; ‹ › move it by half) with the camera's motion (grey) and smart detections (person amber, vehicle blue, animal green) from `/api/cameras/<id>/timeline`, and what Protect no longer keeps shaded (it deletes the oldest when its disk is full: about 80 days at home, 13 at the cabin). Dragging shows stills from the recording (`/cameras/<id>/still`, 640x360); releasing plays from there at full resolution: pieces of 20 s, aligned to multiples of 20 s, exported from Protect (`/cameras/<id>/recording?start=`; about 6 MB and under a second at home, 12 MB and about 4 s from the cabin; `X-Recording-Start` says where its video begins, the keyframe up to 5 s before the start) and fetched whole by the browser, which plays them back to back in two `<video>` elements (the next piece is ready before the current one ends). Back/forward 10 s, pause, 1×/2×/4×, the same moment on the next camera; *Live* or catching up with the present returns to live video, which is closed while a recording plays. *Cameras → Live*: on the cabin's console the tile picture is Protect's snapshot (640x360, about 25 KB, shared by all viewers for 4 s) instead of a go2rtc frame, which costs its mobile link far less. Uses Protect's **private** API (`/proxy/protect/api`, as its web app and Home Assistant do; the official integration API has no event history or recordings), so a Protect update may break it; certificate not verified (self-signed consoles); JSON fetched gzipped (the cabin's events: 80 KB instead of 670 KB). Clips and recording pieces are exported at full resolution (about 0.7 MB per second of video; events over 3 min are cut; at most 2 exports at once per console) into the pod's `/tmp` (kept under 2.5 GB, least recently used out first) and served from there, because Protect's export ignores the Range requests Safari needs. Cards hidden without the login, the cabin's also without its URL; the cabin's events are fetched after the page loads (`src/lib/server/protect.ts`) |
| Google Calendar: the API as a service account (`GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, `CALENDAR_ID`), or the read-only secret iCal address (`CALENDAR_ICS_URL`); all from secret `dashboard-calendar` | Calendar card: the shared *Family* calendar, fetched server-side at most every 5 min (last good copy served for up to an hour if Google fails); day boundaries are Europe/Helsinki. Tap an event for its details (date, time, duration, location, description; Google's HTML descriptions as plain lines). With the service account, **Add** on the card opens a form (name, date, start and duration or all day with a number of days, optional location and description) that creates the event in the calendar (`POST /api/calendar`, wall-clock time in Europe/Helsinki), **Edit** in an event's details opens the same form filled in (`PATCH /api/calendar/<id>`), and **Delete** there asks once more (*Keep* / *Delete*) before removing it (`DELETE /api/calendar/<id>`; an event already deleted elsewhere counts as done). An edit sends Google only the parts that changed, so changing the time keeps a description's formatting (rewriting the description stores it as plain text); it can switch between all-day and timed, and an empty location or description clears it. Editing or deleting one occurrence of a repeating event affects only that occurrence (the form and the question say so); the series is changed in Google Calendar. The card reads through the API (`singleEvents`: Google expands recurring events). Without it, the iCal feed is read and its recurring events are expanded here (with exceptions and cancellations), and there is no Add, Edit or Delete. The address and the key are never sent to the browser or logged. Card colour: `CALENDAR_COLOR` (default `#38bdf8`) |
| Posti (public tracking used by posti.fi: anonymous token from `auth-service.posti.fi`, GraphQL at `graphql.posti.fi`; no account), Matkahuolto (public tracking used by matkahuolto.fi/seuranta: `wwwservice.matkahuolto.fi/search/trackingInfo`; no account), DSV (public tracking used by dsv.com/mydsv/tracking-public: `www.dsv.com/nges-portal/api/public/tracking-public`; no account), UPS Tracking API (`UPS_CLIENT_ID`/`UPS_CLIENT_SECRET` from secret `dashboard-packages`) | Packages card: add a tracking code (carrier detected from its format) with an optional name, or only a name when the code has not come yet (shown as *No code yet*, not checked, never archived automatically; the code is added later in the package's details, `PATCH /api/packages/<id>` with `code`, and a DHL package's 14 days count from then), rename it later with the pencil next to the name in its details (empty = show the code), see status, pickup point and deadline, ETA and the event history; delivered packages move to the archive after 2 days. **Returns**: *Not returned / Partly returned / Returned* in a package's details (`returned`: `partial` | `full` | null, with `returnedAt`) keeps it on the card in its own section, *Returned · refund to check*, below *Incoming* (the headings appear only while something is returned; oldest return first, in amber with *Sent back … · check the refund*; no longer checked, never archived automatically) until *Refund received* archives it; marking an archived package returned puts it back on the card. **Archive**: *Archive* in a package's details takes it off the card (no longer checked); the box button on the card (with the count) opens the archive, newest first, where a package's details open with *Back to the card* and *Delete*; adding an archived code again also puts it back. A package put back is archived automatically again only 2 days (delivered) or 14 days (DHL) after that. The archive keeps the 100 most recently archived. Each package on the card is re-checked while the dashboard is open, at most every 20 min. Card and archive are stored in `/data/packages.json` (PVC `dashboard-data`; archived ones have `archivedAt`). UPS without credentials is shown as "not set up". **Posti**'s status values are undocumented: the state comes from `status.main` (seen: `IN_TRANSPORT`), the pickup point's status and the latest event's text (*noudettavissa* = ready for pickup). The pickup point's status is `PLANNED` as soon as a locker or outlet is reserved, while the parcel is still on its way, so that alone does not count as ready for pickup (`src/lib/server/packages/posti.ts`). **Matkahuolto** codes `MH` + 9 digits + `FI` (or `F` + digit for one parcel of a shipment) are detected; its documented tracking API needs a business contract, so the status is read from the public answer's Finnish event texts, which follow the event list in Matkahuolto's tracking specification (MHTracking_FinV1.3: *Noudettavissa* = ready for pickup, *Luovutettu* = delivered, *Palautettu noutamattomana* = returned); the pickup point is named by its town only, and there is no ETA. Shown as **MH** on the card (`src/lib/server/packages/matkahuolto.ts`). **DSV** (domestic parcels, the former DB Schenker network): 12-digit codes are detected; the parcel is looked up by waybill number, then its road shipment's details. DSV's API answers each request first with 429 and a proof-of-work puzzle (`Captcha-Puzzle`), which its own page solves in the browser and sends back (`Captcha-Solution`); the dashboard does the same (double SHA-256, about 0.1 s per request). The state comes from the progress bar (`BOOKED`, `TRANSPORTATION`, `DISPATCHING_CENTER`, `IN_DELIVERY`, `DELIVERED`) and the latest event's text for pickup points and problems; the answer names no pickup point or deadline, so a parcel at a pickup point shows the latest event's place. Only `BOOKED` has been seen so far; the other steps' and pickup events' texts are unverified (`src/lib/server/packages/dsv.ts`). The official DSV API needs a customer agreement. **DHL is link-only**: its tracking API is for business customers only and dhl.com blocks automated lookups, so DHL packages show "Check at DHL" with a link to DHL's tracking page and move to the archive 14 days after being added (nothing reports them delivered). Aggregators with DHL data (17TRACK, Ship24, Parcel app) were considered: no free plan with lasting API access |

| BluOS players (`BLUOS_PLAYERS`, `site:address:label`; Bluesound NODEs, HTTP on port 11000, no login: Lenbrook's BluOS Custom Integration API v1.7; the cabin's over the Site Magic VPN) | *Home* → Music card (the home players) and *Cabin* → Cabin music card: per player, or per group playing together, the art (the player's own pictures through `/api/music/<id>/image`), title, artist and album or the input (HDMI ARC), back / play-pause / skip (only when the source allows them), mute and volume −/+ in steps of 2; *⋯* shows the sources (the inputs and Spotify from the player's top-level browse menu, then any BluOS presets; *Spotify* resumes the player's Spotify Connect session, the account's last music) and the sleep timer (each tap: 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 min, off). *Play together* groups the site's players under the one playing (else the first listed); *Separate* ungroups. A player switched off shows *Off the network*. A player stuck in *Connecting to Spotify…*, with Spotify not finding it either (seen once, 2026-11-01, on the group's leader), recovers with a restart: `curl -d yes=1 http://<address>/reboot`. The server long-polls each player's `/Status` (100 s, answered at once on any change; a player not answering is retried every minute), so the players see the same requests however many screens are open; commands (`POST /api/music/<id>`) answer with every player's state read back. **Volume cap** `BLUOS_MAX_VOLUME` (30): the dashboard never raises a player above it, and before starting playback (play, a source, grouping, unmute) it lowers any player that would sound louder to it; the BluOS app and the players' buttons are not limited. Each player's volume is its own (`tell_slaves=0`), also in a group |
| apps/telescope (`TELESCOPE_URL`, cluster-internal) | *Telescope* tab: the ASIAIR's status (online, or offline in amber with the last contact), the session (target, frames, integration time) and the running plan from its Autorun log (plan, `current / planned` bar, state and activity, recent events), the newest frame with target, frame number, exposure, gain, sensor temperature and time, *Sky quality* (each frame's sky background, star count and HFR over the session, or without a session over the newest frames' run, same target and mode, titled *Last session* with its target and date; each line on its own scale: background rises with the moon, lit clouds or dawn, stars drop with clouds or dew, HFR rises when focus drifts) and the recent frames. Browser refresh: music 5 s (answered from the server's memory), live data 10 s, presence 30 s, Protect events 30 s (each console), telescope 30 s (only while its tab is open), homelab/speed test/workouts/lists/cabin pictures 60 s, calendar 5 min, weather and tonight's sky 10 min (tonight's sky only while the Telescope tab is open), camera snapshots 5 s (only on *Cameras → Live*; paused at night and while live video is open).

Adding, editing and deleting events needs the Google Calendar API, through a service account the calendar is shared with (no user login that expires):

1. https://console.cloud.google.com: create a project (e.g. *homelab*), then *APIs & Services → Library → Google Calendar API → Enable*.
2. *IAM & Admin → Service Accounts → Create service account* (e.g. `dashboard`; no roles). Open it, *Keys → Add key → Create new key → JSON*; a `.json` file downloads.
3. Google Calendar → the *Family* calendar's *Settings and sharing*: under *Share with specific people*, add the service account's email (`dashboard@<project>.iam.gserviceaccount.com`) with *Make changes to events*. Under *Integrate calendar*, copy the *Calendar ID*.
4. Store both (with the iCal address, which stays until the API is confirmed working), then delete the downloaded key:

   ```sh
   read -rp 'Calendar ID: ' C
   kubectl -n dashboard get secret dashboard-calendar -o json | jq --arg c "$(printf %s "$C" | base64 -w0)" \
     --arg k "$(base64 -w0 < ~/Downloads/<key>.json)" '.data.CALENDAR_ID = $c | .data.GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT = $k' | kubectl apply -f -
   unset C
   kubectl -n dashboard rollout restart deploy/dashboard
   ```

iCal address (Google Calendar → the calendar's *Settings and sharing* → *Secret address in iCal format*), used while the service account is not set up. Resetting it there invalidates the old one; then store the new one:

```sh
read -rsp 'Secret iCal address: ' U && echo
kubectl -n dashboard patch secret dashboard-calendar -p "{\"stringData\":{\"CALENDAR_ICS_URL\":\"$U\"}}"
unset U
kubectl -n dashboard rollout restart deploy/dashboard
```

UPS tracking credentials (optional): an app from https://developer.ups.com (OAuth client credentials with the *Tracking* product):

```sh
read -rsp 'UPS client ID: ' I && echo; read -rsp 'UPS client secret: ' S && echo
kubectl -n dashboard create secret generic dashboard-packages \
  --from-literal=UPS_CLIENT_ID="$I" --from-literal=UPS_CLIENT_SECRET="$S" --dry-run=client -o yaml | kubectl apply -f -
unset I S
kubectl -n dashboard rollout restart deploy/dashboard
```

UniFi Protect login (optional; any local UniFi account that can view Protect, here the read-only account UnPoller uses, see [platform/README.md](../../platform/README.md)):

```sh
kubectl -n unpoller get secret unifi-readonly -o json | jq '{apiVersion: "v1", kind: "Secret", type: "Opaque",
  metadata: {name: "dashboard-protect", namespace: "dashboard"}, data: {PROTECT_USER: .data.user, PROTECT_PASS: .data.pass}}' | kubectl apply -f -
kubectl -n dashboard rollout restart deploy/dashboard
```

After changing that account's password, update `unifi-readonly` and run the above again.

## Access

Two addresses: http://dashboard.lab.internal at home (or over the UDM's VPN), and https://dashboard.shillyshally.org from anywhere, through the Cloudflare Tunnel behind Cloudflare Access (email one-time code; the Access policy *Family*, shared with apps/notify, apps/news and the telescope web app; `/manifest.webmanifest` and `/icons/*` bypass Access so iOS can add it to the Home Screen). Cards and links to other apps (lists, workout, homelab) open their `lab.internal` addresses, which work only at home; the telescope app link follows the dashboard's address. Notifications open the public address (`DASHBOARD_APP_URL`).

**No login of its own**: Cloudflare Access is the only protection on the public address, and on the home network (and the UDM's VPN) the dashboard is open to every device, cameras included. The live video relay (`server.js`) accepts a WebSocket only when its `Origin` is the dashboard's own address, so another website a family member visits cannot open a camera through their browser; other cross-site requests cannot read the responses (no CORS), and SvelteKit refuses cross-site form posts.

## Configuration

Non-secret settings are literals in `deploy/kustomization.yaml` (`dashboard-config`): cameras (`id:label`, ids = go2rtc stream names, in tile order), the Protect consoles and their cameras (`name:label:CAMERAS id`; the id links the camera's tile and live video to its recordings), weather locations, the telescope site, the BluOS players (`site:address:label`) and the dashboard's volume cap, service URLs. Apply with `kubectl apply -k apps/dashboard/deploy`. A new cabin Protect camera needs an entry in `CAMERAS`, in `CABIN_PROTECT_CAMERAS` and in go2rtc ([platform/README.md](../../platform/README.md) step 8).

## Develop

```sh
cd apps/dashboard
npm install
kubectl -n go2rtc port-forward svc/go2rtc 1984 &   # go2rtc is cluster-internal
PROMETHEUS_URL=http://prometheus.lab.internal GO2RTC_URL=http://localhost:1984 \
  CAMERAS='front-door:Front door,backyard:Backyard,carport:Carport' npm run dev
npm run check   # svelte-check / TypeScript
```

`npm run dev` serves everything except live video (the WebSocket relay lives in `server.js`); for that, `npm run build && node server.js` with the same environment plus `ORIGIN=http://localhost:3000`.

## Deploy

```sh
./apps/dashboard/deploy.sh
```

Builds with podman, imports the image into k3s's containerd (`sudo k3s ctr`; no registry, `imagePullPolicy: Never`), writes the tag into `deploy/kustomization.yaml`, applies, and waits for the rollout. Commit the tag change.

`src/lib/vendor/go2rtc/video-rtc.js` is go2rtc 1.9.14's player (MIT, see `LICENSE` there).
